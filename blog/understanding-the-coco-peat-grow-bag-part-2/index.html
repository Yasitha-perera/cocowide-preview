Understanding the Coco Peat Grow Bag (Part 2)

A typical coco peat grow bag is a self-contained, ready-to-use cultivation system widely used in hydroponics and greenhouses. It consists of two primary parts:

Part 1: The Outer Bag (Plastic Sleeve)

The bag is engineered to protect the root zone and facilitate plant growth. Made from UV-stabilized LDPE or polypropylene film, the co-extruded “black-and-white” design reflects sunlight to keep roots cool on the outside, while the black inner layer blocks light to prevent algae growth. Each bag comes with pre-cut planting holes for seedlings (typically 4 to 6 per bag), dripper holes for irrigation stakes, and drainage slits to prevent waterlogging. The dimensions (L x W x H) and bag gauge are determined by the intended crop, growing period, and required substrate volume.

Part 2: The Internal Substrate Mix (Growing Medium)

The filling is a compressed slab of materials that expands upon hydration:
🟤 Coco Peat (Coir Pith): The base material providing high water retention
🟤 Coco Husk Chips: Larger pieces of dried husk added to increase aeration and improve drainage

The selection of the substrate mix is a decision to be taken considering the crop, fertigation system and climatic conditions.

For professional use, the internal medium is treated to meet specific standards:
🟢 Buffered Substrate: Treated with calcium and magnesium to prevent nutrient lockout and lower natural salt levels
🟢 Low EC (Washed): Washed multiple times with fresh water to remove salts that can damage sensitive roots
🟢 Optimal pH: Naturally ranges between 5.2 and 6.5, ideal for most agricultural crops
